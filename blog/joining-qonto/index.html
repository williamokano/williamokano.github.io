A new chapter: joining Qonto

August 16, 2026 · 2 min read

careerfintech

After a run at Grammarly, I have joined Qonto's Wealth & Market team as a Staff Software Engineer, working on the investment side of the product.

Also available in: Português, Deutsch

I left Grammarly at the end of June 2026. In July I started at Qonto as a Staff Software Engineer on Wealth & Market — the team behind the investment side of the product. There were a couple of weeks off in between, which was exactly as much time as I needed to stop thinking about the last codebase.

Why this, and why now#

I spent the last stretch of my career at the intersection of AI and productivity tooling, and I learned a lot there about building for scale when the product changes underneath you every quarter. But the problems that have always pulled hardest at me are the ones where the cost of being wrong is concrete.

Money is that kind of problem. I first ran into it properly at N26, and the lesson stuck: a failed transaction is not a bug report, it is a broken promise. Investments raise the stakes again — you inherit correctness, regulation, and customer trust, and they all land on the same code path.

What Wealth & Market does#

Qonto is the business account a few hundred thousand European companies run their finances on. Wealth & Market is the part of that story where a company's idle cash stops being idle: savings, investment products, the plumbing that moves money into them and reports on it accurately afterwards.

It is a good domain for the kind of engineering I like. The constraints are real and written down by someone other than me. The correctness bar is not negotiable. And the systems have to be legible to the next engineer, because in this domain "we will remember why we did that" is not a plan.

What "staff" means to me#

The title changes less about the work than people expect. I still read the code and I still get paged. What changes is the radius: more of my week goes into decisions that other people will build on for a year, and into making sure those decisions are written down somewhere findable.

The short version of how I try to work:

  • Start with the actual constraint, not the preferred technology.
  • Be boring on the critical path, adventurous everywhere it is cheap to be wrong.
  • Optimise for the team's throughput, not my own.
  • Stay attached to a design through its first incident — that is when it tells you the truth.

What I will write here#

This blog is new. I plan to use it for the things that do not fit in a pull request description: notes on distributed systems, the occasional post-mortem written for strangers, and what fifteen years of backend work has taught me about the parts of the job that are not code.

Posts will be in English, Portuguese, and German — though not always all three. If a post exists in your language, the site will show it to you; if not, you will get the original with a small badge saying so.

More soon.
